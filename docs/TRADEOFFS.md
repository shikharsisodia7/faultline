# Tradeoffs and known limitations

## Deliberate choices

- **Deterministic correlation, no LLM in the core.** Explainability and reproducibility matter more than a model's fluency when the output feeds a postmortem. The AI investigator is optional and must cite evidence.
- **Heuristic scores, labeled as such.** Correlation and ranking scores are bounded in [0, 1] but uncalibrated. The UI never calls them probabilities.
- **Synthetic incidents generated by the simulator.** Their logs and their counterfactual baseline cannot disagree, which keeps the demo honest. The cost is that the demo data is only as realistic as the model.
- **Server actions as the only write path.** Slightly more code than client mutations, but it closes cross-workspace injection and gives one place to audit.
- **Events as individual records with a 5,000-event cap.** Live sync and per-row RBAC come for free; very large incidents would need a different store (see Architecture).
- **One canonical event model.** Format-specific detail is kept in `metadata`; nothing is lost, but formats are not modeled natively.
- **Hand-built SVG visualizations.** The graph, timeline, and charts are small, dependency-free components tuned for this product, rather than general charting libraries.

## Known limitations

- The simulator models one request type with constant network latency and no partitions, autoscaling, or deadline propagation (timeouts never cancel downstream work).
- Counterfactuals for imported incidents use default capacities and traffic; they are hypothetical by construction and say so.
- Text-log parsing covers the documented rules; anything else is reported as unparseable rather than guessed.
- Presence rooms are keyed by unguessable incident ids but do not check workspace membership (they carry names and playheads only).
- Hypothesis reviews are a simple tally; there is no weighting by reviewer or required quorum.
- Correlation weights are hand-chosen defaults, not learned from labeled incidents.
- The standalone Vercel build cannot sign in, sync, or store anything outside the browser.
- External integrations bill the app owner's DeepSpace credits and are disabled by default.
